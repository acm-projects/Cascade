# Cascade editing direction from INSPO

Reviewed 13 November 2026: all 28 Desktop/INSPO videos, sampled across their full duration at 0.5–2.13 second intervals. This is visual sequence review, not continuous playback or listening. Fast transitions need finer samples; audio claims below are design instructions, not claims of listening. Original reference footage and creator branding must never appear in a Cascade export.

## Direction used by the planner

Choose a visual language from the content and request, then name it in the reply. Vary the hook, hero-word treatment, shot rhythm and ending between edits; do not run one fixed sequence of presets. Use these as craft references, not as instructions to invent facts or irrelevant visuals.

- **Kinetic conversation:** strongest complete statement or question first; punch between 1.0 and 1.12–1.2 on a new idea, not on every word. Preserve Q → A when the question matters. Keep faces in sync and use 2–4 second holds between bursts. Pick 2–4 meaningful nouns, verbs or numbers for giant behind/outline words, with an occasional slam, circled word, or short RGB/whip accent. Small readable captions carry the rest. Do not apply effects to filler words.
- **Editorial collage:** evidence-led B-roll tied to the exact spoken noun, cut on its word; cream/grid photo cards with slight opposing tilts, ink reveals and one drawn circle around a claim. Heavy grotesk plus italic serif. Use actual objects from the story; never change an animal, person or location just to find stock. Avoid treating illustrative stock as documentary evidence.
- **Atmospheric cinema:** longer 3–5 second holds, slow 1.0→1.06 pushes, color matched to the topic, a quiet serif payoff against a designed burst earlier. A text-mask opener only when matching footage is available. One ripple/ink transition can carry the surprise; keep calm sections calm.
- **Contrast makes the standout:** for ordinary creative requests, plan at least two different designed moments on clips over 12 s, spaced apart, one early and one near the turn/payoff. On shorter clips, one clear moment is enough. Explicit requests for minimal edits or no effects take priority. Avoid repeated slams, constant shake, strobing or gratuitous transitions. A quiet hold can be the contrast between two bold moments.
- **Typography:** 2–5 word groups; one semantic hero, heavy sans paired with italic serif; giant words behind a real subject matte, outline in front, face unobscured. Circled/scribble words are hand-drawn-looking emphasis. Perspective titles are a designed screen-space plane, never pretend to be motion-tracked to a wall. Colors by meaning: water blue, life green, danger red, money gold, cream otherwise. Stay within 80% title-safe.
- **Picture and transitions:** use source cutaways first, then concrete stock. Incoming shots 1.5–3 seconds for dialogue, and a photo-card/band only when the composition benefits. Accent events are word-number anchored: whip (short directional blur/settle), RGB (channel separation resolving cleanly), ink (irregular wipe from previous picture), ripple (Cascade water refraction). Use 0.18–0.5 s and space them at least 1.5 s apart. Do not cover the spoken payoff with a new unrelated shot.
- **Sound and sync:** speech controls cut timing; arrange the score around it. Protect complete words, duck music under voice, use measured onsets/word timestamps, a short silence before one payoff, sub hit on its return, whoosh only on a real visual change. Never claim to have heard contact sheets. Existing local sound synthesis supplies accents; no fabricated commercial soundtrack or unlicensed reference audio.
- **Color:** warm skin, cool shadows; forest green only when forest is actually relevant. Near-monochrome for a dark claim can contrast with color returning at resolution. Preserve the user's aspect ratio unless asked to change it.
- **Cascade signature:** water/ripple visual language, cream and water-blue accents, a concise earned closing line, then the existing liquid handwritten Cascade stamp. User requests for no sign-off win. Do not attach a generic moral or call to action.
